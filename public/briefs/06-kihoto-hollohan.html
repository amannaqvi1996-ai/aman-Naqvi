AOR Paper IVLeading Case 6 of 86Master Brief

Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu

The anti-defection law survived — but the clause that locked the courts out did not. The Speaker decides defection cases as a tribunal, and a tribunal answers to judicial review.

1992 Supp (2) SCC 651AIR 1993 SC 412[1992] 1 SCR 68618 February 19925 Judges · 3:2

Read this first — where the Bench agreed and where it split

All five Judges agreed that Paragraph 7 of the Tenth Schedule ousts the courts and therefore needed ratification by the States under the proviso to Article 368(2) — which it never got. They split 3:2 on the consequence. The majority severed Paragraph 7 and saved the rest. The minority held the whole 52nd Amendment void. Learn the case as: same defect, different remedy.

Later law. Paragraph 3 (split by one-third) was deleted by the 91st Amendment, 2003. Timelines for the Speaker came with Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020), which is case 59 on your list. Neither is part of this judgment.

On sources. No judgment file was uploaded. I worked from the Supreme Court's head-notes volume and the settled reported position. §30 lists what I could not verify.

02If I Have Only 60 Seconds

Facts → Issue → Rule → Ratio → Holding → Provision → Significance

  1. Law. The 52nd Amendment, 1985 added the Tenth Schedule — disqualification of legislators for defection, decided by the Speaker or Chairman.
  2. Challenge. A batch of petitions, including from Nagaland, attacked the Schedule as destroying free speech in the House, democracy and judicial review.
  3. Issue. Is the Tenth Schedule valid? Is the Speaker's decision open to judicial review, given the finality clause (Para 6) and the bar on courts (Para 7)?
  4. Ratio. Para 7 changes Articles 136, 226 and 227 in effect; it needed State ratification; lacking it, Para 7 is invalid but severable. The rest of the Schedule is valid.
  5. Speaker. Acting under Para 6 the Speaker is a tribunal; the decision is subject to judicial review on limited grounds.
  6. Timing. No review before the Speaker decides (no quia timet action), save for interim disqualification or suspension with grave, immediate and irreversible consequences.
  7. Significance. The governing authority on anti-defection law and on severing part of a constitutional amendment.

03One-Line Ratio

The sentence to reproduce

The ratio in one line

The Tenth Schedule is valid except Paragraph 7, which is void for want of ratification under the proviso to Article 368(2) and is severable; the Speaker or Chairman deciding under Paragraph 6 is a tribunal whose decision is subject to judicial review under Articles 136, 226 and 227 for violation of constitutional mandate, mala fides, non-compliance with natural justice and perversity. Certain

04Facts

What actually happened

  • 1985. Parliament enacted the 52nd Amendment, adding Articles 102(2) and 191(2) and the Tenth Schedule. The Bill was not sent to the States for ratification. Certain
  • Para 2 disqualifies a member who (a) voluntarily gives up membership of the party, or (b) votes or abstains contrary to the party's direction without permission and without condonation in 15 days.
  • Para 6 makes the Speaker or Chairman the deciding authority, the decision "final", and deems the proceedings to be proceedings in the House under Articles 122 and 212.
  • Para 7 barred every court from any jurisdiction in respect of disqualification under the Schedule.
  • The litigation. Members facing or resisting disqualification in several States, including Nagaland, came to the Supreme Court and High Courts; the matters were heard together. Strong

08Ratio Decidendi

Five propositions

1 — Para 7 required ratification

Rule. A provision that in terms and in effect takes away the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and High Courts changes Articles 136, 226 and 227 and falls within the proviso to Article 368(2). Result. Para 7, never ratified, is invalid. Unanimous. Certain

2 — Para 7 is severable

Rule. The doctrine of severability applies to constitutional amendments. The proviso to Article 368(2) does not make the whole Bill void when only one provision needed ratification. Reasoning. Para 7 is independent; the remaining Paragraphs are complete and workable without it. Result. Rest of the Schedule stands. Majority only. Certain

3 — Para 2 is constitutional

Rule. Disqualification for defection does not violate the freedom of speech of members under Articles 105 and 194, nor democracy. Reasoning. Political parties are integral to parliamentary democracy; unprincipled floor-crossing undermines it; the law seeks to strengthen, not subvert, the democratic fabric. Narrowing. "Any direction" in Para 2(1)(b) is confined to (i) a vote on a motion of confidence or no-confidence, and (ii) matters integral to the party's policy and programme on which it went to the electorate. A direction should be worded to warn that defiance invites disqualification. Certain

4 — The Speaker is a tribunal

Rule. The power under Para 6(1) is judicial; the Speaker or Chairman exercising it is a tribunal. Result. The decision is amenable to Articles 136, 226 and 227. The "finality" clause does not oust that jurisdiction. Vesting the power in the Speaker is not unconstitutional, given the high office. Certain

5 — Scope and timing of review

Grounds. Jurisdictional errors only: infirmities based on violation of constitutional mandate, mala fides, non-compliance with rules of natural justice, and perversity. Certain Timing. No judicial review at a stage prior to the Speaker's decision; no quia timet action. Exception: interlocutory disqualification or suspension likely to have grave, immediate and irreversible repercussions. Certain Para 6(2). The deeming fiction gives only the immunity of Articles 122(1) and 212(1) — against mere irregularity of procedure. Certain

09Obiter & Observations

What is not binding

  • The majority's reflections on the role of political parties and on the dignity of the Speaker's office are reasoning, not rule. Certain
  • The distinction suggested between the Speaker and the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (the Vice-President) is an observation. Strong
  • The minority's view that an independent body should decide defection cases is not law — though it was echoed, as a recommendation to Parliament, in Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020). Strong

12Precedent Chain

Where the case sits in the line

Later developments — not part of this judgment

  • 91st Amendment, 2003 removed the "split" defence (Para 3) and capped the size of Councils of Ministers. Merger (Para 4, two-thirds) remains. Certain
  • Nabam Rebia (2016) held that a Speaker facing a removal notice should not decide disqualification petitions; that proposition was referred to a larger Bench in Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Governor of Maharashtra (2023). Strong
  • Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020) — case 59 on the list — read a time limit into Para 6 and suggested Parliament consider an independent tribunal. Strong

13Dissent & Concurring Opinions

The minority opinion

Verma J., for himself and Sharma J.: Certain

  • The Bill required ratification because of Para 7. Ratification is a step before assent; without it the President's assent is non est.
  • Severability cannot rescue a constitutional amendment that was never validly made. The whole 52nd Amendment is unconstitutional.
  • Independently, making the Speaker — whose tenure depends on the continued support of the majority — the sole arbiter violates a basic feature: adjudication by an independent authority.
  • On the points of agreement: the finality clause alone does not oust Articles 136, 226 and 227; Para 6(2) cannot be stretched beyond Articles 122(1) and 212(1).

14Final Holding

The operative result

  • Para 7 of the Tenth Schedule is invalid for want of ratification. Certain
  • Para 7 is severable; the rest of the Tenth Schedule is valid. Certain
  • Para 2 does not violate any basic feature, or Articles 105 and 194. Certain
  • The Speaker or Chairman is a tribunal; decisions are subject to judicial review on the stated grounds. Certain
  • No review before decision, save the narrow interim exception. Certain

16AOR-Specific Importance

Why an AOR candidate must own this case

  • Maintainability. A writ challenging pending disqualification proceedings is premature — plead the exception (interim suspension with irreversible effect) or wait for the order.
  • Grounds. Draft the challenge under the four heads only: constitutional mandate, mala fides, natural justice, perversity. A plea that the Speaker "erred on facts" will not do.
  • Forum. Article 226 before the High Court, or Article 136; the Speaker's order is that of a tribunal.
  • Severability of amendments. Authority for saving the valid part of a constitutional amendment.
  • Ratification. The test for when the proviso to Article 368(2) is attracted: change "in terms and in effect" to the entrenched provisions.
  • Links. Keisham Meghachandra (59), Bommai (floor test), Rameshwar Prasad (22).

172-Mark Questions

Answer in two or three lines

Q1Which Paragraph of the Tenth Schedule was struck down and why?

Paragraph 7 — it ousted the jurisdiction of all courts, thereby changing Articles 136, 226 and 227, and was not ratified by the States as the proviso to Article 368(2) requires.

Q2What is the status of the Speaker under Paragraph 6?

A tribunal exercising judicial power; the decision is subject to judicial review.

Q3State the four grounds of judicial review.

Violation of constitutional mandate; mala fides; non-compliance with rules of natural justice; perversity.

Q4What is a quia timet action and is it permitted?

A pre-emptive action brought in anticipation of an adverse decision. Not permitted against the Speaker, except where interim disqualification or suspension has grave, immediate and irreversible consequences.

Q5How was "any direction" in Para 2(1)(b) read?

As confined to votes on confidence or no-confidence motions and matters integral to the party's policy and programme placed before the electorate.

Q6Bench and vote?

Five Judges; 3:2.

185-Mark Questions

Answer in about 150 words

Q1Explain the majority's reasoning on severability.

All five Judges held that Para 7 required ratification. The majority held that the proviso to Article 368(2) attaches only to the provision that makes the change in the entrenched Articles, not to the whole Bill. The doctrine of severability, used for ordinary legislation, applies equally to a constitutional amendment. Para 7 is independent of the rest; Paras 1 to 6 and 8 form a complete, workable code without it; and Parliament would have enacted them even without Para 7. Therefore only Para 7 falls. The minority disagreed: ratification is a condition precedent to assent, so the Bill as a whole was never validly assented to and nothing remained to sever.

Q2Does the Tenth Schedule violate freedom of speech of legislators?

No. Articles 105 and 194 protect what a member says or how he votes from proceedings in court; they do not guarantee freedom from the constitutional consequence of defection. A member elected on a party ticket owes loyalty to its programme; the law aims to curb unprincipled defections that undermine democracy. The Court narrowed "any direction" so that ordinary dissent is not penalised.

Q3What does the deeming clause in Para 6(2) achieve?

It treats proceedings under Para 6(1) as proceedings in the House under Articles 122 and 212. Those Articles bar courts from questioning proceedings for irregularity of procedure only. They do not protect illegality or unconstitutionality. So Para 6(2) does not oust review.

Q4State the minority view.

Verma and Sharma JJ. held the entire 52nd Amendment unconstitutional — for want of ratification, which cannot be cured by severance — and held further that vesting adjudication in the Speaker, who depends on the majority, violates the basic feature of independent adjudication.

19Long-Answer Questions

Structure for a 15–20 mark answer

Q1Critically examine the scope of judicial review over the Speaker's decision under the Tenth Schedule.

Introduction. Tenth Schedule; Paras 6 and 7; Kihoto Hollohan. Para 7. Ouster; ratification; unanimous invalidity; severed. Speaker as tribunal. Judicial power; Arts. 136, 226, 227. Finality and Para 6(2). Neither ousts review. Grounds. Four heads; jurisdictional errors only. Timing. After decision; interim exception. Later cases. Ravi Naik; Rajendra Singh Rana; Keisham Meghachandra; Subhash Desai. Critique. Speaker's partisanship; delay; minority view vindicated? Conclusion. Review exists but is narrow and after the event.

Q2"Same defect, different remedy." Explain the difference between the majority and minority in Kihoto Hollohan.

Common ground. Para 7 needed ratification; finality clause; Para 6(2). Majority. Severability; Para 2 valid; Speaker valid adjudicator. Minority. Assent non est; whole Amendment void; Speaker violates basic feature. Which is sounder? Text of proviso; legislative intent; practical consequences. Conclusion.

22Top 10 Exam Traps

Ten ways to lose marks

  1. Saying Para 7 was struck down for violating the basic structure — the majority's ground is non-ratification.
  2. Saying the Tenth Schedule was struck down.
  3. Treating the Speaker's decision as immune because of "finality".
  4. Listing "error of law" or "error of fact" among the grounds.
  5. Giving the 3:2 split as the vote on Para 7.
  6. Citing the three-month time limit as part of Kihoto.
  7. Writing about the split defence as current law.
  8. Forgetting the narrowing of "any direction".
  9. Saying courts can never intervene before the decision — there is an exception.
  10. Naming the wrong author — the majority is Venkatachaliah J., not Verma J.

25Memory Architecture

How to hold it in memory

  • "Seven is severed." Para 7 goes; the rest stays.
  • Four grounds: C-M-N-P. Constitutional mandate, Mala fides, Natural justice, Perversity.
  • After, not before. Review follows the Speaker's order.
  • Speaker = tribunal. Tribunal = Articles 136, 226, 227.
  • Irregular yes, illegal no. That is all Para 6(2) protects.
  • 52 in, 91 out. 52nd put the Schedule in; 91st took Para 3 out.
