Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra

The case that invented the curative petition. A final judgment of the Supreme Court cannot be attacked by a writ under Article 32 — but in the rarest of cases the Court will reopen it under its own inherent power.

(2002) 4 SCC 388AIR 2002 SC 1771[2002] 2 SCR 100610 April 20025 Judges · unanimous, two opinions

Read this first — this is a Paper I case as much as a Paper IV case

For an Advocate-on-Record this judgment is working procedure. The curative petition it created is now written into the Supreme Court Rules, 2013, Order XLVIII. Strong Expect it in Paper I (Practice and Procedure) and Paper II (Drafting) as well as here. The judgment refers to the 1966 Rules (Order XLVII Rule 6 — inherent powers); under the 2013 Rules the inherent-power rule is Order LV Rule 6. Strong

Two answers in one case. Question 1 — is a writ under Article 32 maintainable against a final judgment of this Court? No. Question 2 — is there any other way to reopen it after review is dismissed? Yes — curative petition, under inherent power. Always give both.

On sources. No judgment file was uploaded. I worked from the Supreme Court's head-notes volume and the settled reported position. §30 lists what I could not verify.

02If I Have Only 60 Seconds

Facts → Issue → Rule → Ratio → Holding → Provision → Significance

  1. Background. In a matrimonial case the Supreme Court had granted a divorce by mutual consent although the wife had withdrawn her consent. Her review petition failed.
  2. Move. She filed a writ petition under Article 32 challenging the Court's own final judgment. Other petitioners in unrelated cases did the same.
  3. Reference. A three-judge Bench referred to a Constitution Bench the question whether such a writ lies.
  4. Rule 1. No. A final judgment of the Supreme Court cannot be assailed under Article 32. Superior courts' judicial orders do not violate fundamental rights, and superior courts are not "State" under Article 12.
  5. Rule 2. But the Court, to prevent abuse of its process and cure gross miscarriage of justice, may reconsider its judgment in exercise of inherent power — ex debito justitiae.
  6. Conditions. Grounds: breach of natural justice, or undisclosed bias of a Judge. Procedure: review grounds repeated; Senior Advocate's certificate; circulation to the three senior-most Judges plus the judgment's authors.
  7. Significance. The last remedy in Indian law.

03One-Line Ratio

The sentence to reproduce

The ratio in one line

A final judgment or order of the Supreme Court cannot be challenged under Article 32; but after dismissal of a review petition the Court may, in exercise of its inherent power and ex debito justitiae, entertain a curative petition to prevent abuse of its process and cure a gross miscarriage of justice, on grounds such as violation of natural justice or bias, subject to strict procedural safeguards. Certain

04Facts

What actually happened

  • The earlier case. In Ashok Hurra v. Rupa Bipin Zaveri (1997), the Supreme Court dissolved the marriage by mutual consent under section 13B of the Hindu Marriage Act, using Article 142, although the wife had withdrawn her consent after the statutory period. Strong
  • Review. The wife's review petition was dismissed. Strong
  • Writ. She then filed a petition under Article 32 questioning the validity of that final judgment. Certain
  • Others. Several other writ petitions raising the same threshold question — challenge to a final judgment of this Court after review — were pending. Strong
  • Reference. A three-judge Bench, noting the importance of the question, referred it to a Constitution Bench. Strong
  • What the Constitution Bench did not do. It did not re-examine the merits of the divorce. It answered the question of law. Strong

08Ratio Decidendi

Two rules and a procedure

Rule 1 — No Article 32 writ against a final judgment

Reasoning. Certain

  • Article 32 exists to enforce fundamental rights. A judicial order of a superior court, made in a matter properly before it, cannot be said to violate a fundamental right (Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar).
  • Superior courts of justice do not fall within "State" or "other authorities" in Article 12.
  • A writ cannot issue from one Bench of this Court to another, nor to a co-ordinate or superior court; nor will this Court issue an Article 32 writ to a High Court against its judicial order — that is corrected in appeal under Articles 132, 133, 134 or 136.
  • Finality of litigation is a matter of public policy.

Rule 2 — Reconsideration under inherent power

Rule. The Court is not powerless to correct its own error. To prevent abuse of its process and to cure a gross miscarriage of justice, it may reconsider its judgment in exercise of inherent power. Balance. Certainty and finality matter, but in the rarest of rare cases the duty to do justice must prevail over the policy of finality. Label. Such a petition is called a curative petition. Certain

Grounds — ex debito justitiae

The petitioner is entitled to relief if he establishes: Certain

  1. Violation of the principles of natural justice — he was not a party to the lis but the judgment adversely affected his interests; or, though a party, he was not served with notice and the matter proceeded as if he had been; or
  2. Apprehension of bias — a Judge who heard the matter failed to disclose his connection with the subject-matter or the parties, and the judgment adversely affects the petitioner.

The Court said it is neither advisable nor possible to enumerate all grounds. Strong Banerjee J. states the bases as natural justice, want of jurisdiction, and loss of public confidence through a Judge's association with the dispute. Certain

Procedure

  1. The petition must aver specifically that the grounds were taken in the review petition and that it was dismissed by circulation. Certain
  2. It must contain a certification by a Senior Advocate as to fulfilment of the requirements. Certain
  3. It is first circulated to a Bench of the three senior-most Judges and the Judges who passed the judgment complained of, if available. Certain
  4. Only if a majority of those Judges conclude that the matter needs hearing is it listed — as far as possible before the same Bench. Certain
  5. The Bench may at any stage ask a senior counsel to assist as amicus curiae. Certain
  6. If the petition is without merit and vexatious, the Court may impose exemplary costs. Certain

09Obiter & Observations

What is not binding

  • The comparative survey — including the House of Lords reopening its own decision in the Pinochet case for apparent bias — is supporting reasoning. Strong
  • Banerjee J.'s broader statement of the grounds (including lack of jurisdiction) is a concurring formulation; the two grounds in Quadri J.'s opinion are the ones the lead opinion specifies. Certain
  • No view was expressed on the merits of the underlying matrimonial judgment. Strong

10Judicial Reasoning

How the Court reasoned

  1. Is Article 32 available? Start from Mirajkar (nine Judges): judicial orders do not infringe fundamental rights. Confirmed in Antulay and Triveniben.
  2. Then is there no remedy at all? Review under Article 137 is the constitutional remedy, but it is limited and usually disposed of by circulation; a second review is barred.
  3. Finality versus justice. Interest reipublicae ut sit finis litium — but no legal system can let its highest court be the instrument of injustice which it has no power to undo.
  4. Source. The Court is a court of record with plenary power under Article 142 and inherent power preserved by Order XLVII Rule 6. Antulay itself recalled directions given without jurisdiction.
  5. Floodgates. A new stage of litigation would be opened for every losing party; hence the strict filters — limited grounds, Senior Advocate's certificate, senior-most Judges, costs.
  6. Name. To distinguish it from review, the petition is styled "curative".

12Precedent Chain

Where the case sits in the line

Later developments — not part of this judgment

  • Supreme Court Rules, 2013, Order XLVIII — "Curative Petition": governed by this judgment; averment and Senior Advocate's certificate required; circulated first to the three senior-most Judges and the Judges concerned; disposed of ordinarily by circulation without oral argument unless ordered. Strong
  • Open-court hearing. Following Mohd. Arif v. Registrar, Supreme Court (2014), review petitions in death cases are heard in open court by three Judges; curative petitions remain by circulation unless the Judges direct otherwise. Strong
  • Examples. Navneet Kaur v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2014) — death sentence commuted on a curative petition; Union of India v. Union Carbide (2023) — curative petition for enhanced Bhopal compensation dismissed, with emphasis on the narrowness of the jurisdiction; Delhi Metro Rail Corporation v. Delhi Airport Metro Express (2024) — curative petition allowed and an arbitral award set aside. Strong

  • Locates the remedy in the concept of justice itself: ex debito justitiae; inherent power recognised by Order XLVII Rule 6 of the 1966 Rules.

14Final Holding

The operative result

  • A writ petition under Article 32 assailing a final judgment or order of this Court is not maintainable. Certain
  • After dismissal of a review petition, a curative petition may be entertained under the Court's inherent power, on the grounds and by the procedure stated. Certain

16AOR-Specific Importance

Why an AOR candidate must own this case

  • Filing checklist for a curative petition. (1) Review dismissed — by circulation; (2) specific averment that the grounds were taken in review; (3) certificate of a Senior Advocate; (4) grounds limited to natural justice or bias (or the like); (5) certificate by the AOR that it is the first curative petition, as the Rules require. Strong
  • Sequence of remedies. SLP/appeal → review (Article 137, Order XLVII of the 2013 Rules, 30 days) → curative (Order XLVIII). No second review. No Article 32 writ.
  • Costs warning. Advise the client that a vexatious curative petition invites exemplary costs.
  • Drafting. Paper II may ask for a curative petition — reproduce the averments from this case in the body and annex the certificate.
  • Article 32 practice. A writ petition disguised as a challenge to a Supreme Court order will be refused registration or dismissed in limine on this authority.
  • Links. A.R. Antulay (5), Supreme Court AOR Association (36), Union of India v. V. Sriharan (37).

172-Mark Questions

Answer in two or three lines

Q1Is a writ under Article 32 maintainable against a final judgment of the Supreme Court?

No. Judicial orders of superior courts do not violate fundamental rights and superior courts are not "State" under Article 12.

Q2What is a curative petition?

A petition, after dismissal of review, invoking the Court's inherent power to reconsider its final judgment to prevent abuse of process and cure gross miscarriage of justice.

Q3State the two grounds specified in the lead opinion.

Violation of natural justice (non-party affected, or party not served); and a Judge's failure to disclose a connection giving rise to apprehension of bias.

Q4Who must certify a curative petition?

A Senior Advocate.

Q5To whom is a curative petition first circulated?

The three senior-most Judges and the Judges who passed the judgment, if available.

Q6Bench and authors?

Five Judges; Quadri J. (lead) and Banerjee J. (concurring); unanimous.

185-Mark Questions

Answer in about 150 words

Q1Why does Article 32 not lie against a judgment of the Supreme Court?

Article 32 is a remedy for enforcement of fundamental rights against the State. In Naresh Mirajkar nine Judges held that a judicial decision of a court of competent jurisdiction, even if erroneous, does not infringe fundamental rights; its correction lies in appeal or review. Superior courts acting judicially are not "State" or "other authorities" within Article 12. Further, a writ of certiorari issues to inferior courts and tribunals, not to courts of co-ordinate or superior jurisdiction; one Bench of the Supreme Court cannot issue a writ to another. Finally, public policy demands finality. For these reasons a final judgment of the Court cannot be reopened under Article 32, whether the petitioner was a party or not.

Q2What is the source of the power to entertain a curative petition?

The inherent power of the Supreme Court as the apex court and a court of record, to prevent abuse of its process and to do justice — recognised, not conferred, by Order XLVII Rule 6 of the Supreme Court Rules, 1966, and supported by Article 142. The governing idea is ex debito justitiae: the Court owes a debt of justice and will correct its own grave error.

Q3Set out the procedural safeguards.

Specific averment that the grounds were urged in review and the review was dismissed by circulation; certificate by a Senior Advocate; circulation to the three senior-most Judges and the authors of the judgment; listing only if a majority so decide, before the same Bench as far as possible; power to appoint amicus; exemplary costs for vexatious petitions.

Q4How does a curative petition differ from a review petition?
  • Review: Article 137; grounds in Order XLVII (error apparent, etc.); within 30 days; before the same Bench; one only.
  • Curative: inherent power; after review fails; grounds of natural justice or bias; Senior Advocate's certificate; three senior-most Judges plus the Bench; exceptional.

27Last-Night Revision Card

The section to read walking in — nothing else

Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra, (2002) 4 SCC 388 — 10 April 2002 — 5 Judges, unanimous

Facts. After the Supreme Court's final judgment in a matrimonial appeal and dismissal of review, the wife filed an Art. 32 writ against that judgment. Three Judges referred the question to a Constitution Bench.

Issues. (1) Does Art. 32 lie against a final judgment of this Court? (2) If not, can the Court reopen it after review?

(2) Yes — under inherent power, ex debito justitiae, to prevent abuse of process and cure gross miscarriage of justice: the curative petition.
